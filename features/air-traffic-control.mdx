## Weather Information

Access real-time aviation weather data for informed flight planning decisions.

### Weather Data Types

- **METAR**: Current weather observations from airports and stations
- **TAF**: Terminal Aerodrome Forecasts for destination planning
- **NOTAMs**: Notices to Airmen about hazards and operational changes

### Searching Weather Data

The weather search panel supports multiple search strategies:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="By Location">
    Search weather using ICAO or FAA airport codes:
    
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Select Location Search">
        Choose the "Location" tab in the search panel.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Enter Airport Code">
        Type the ICAO code (e.g., MMTO, MMMX) or FAA code for the airport.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Search">
        Click "Search" to retrieve current METAR and TAF data.
      </Step>
      <Step title="View Results">
        Results display in table format with decoded weather information.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
  
  <Tab title="By Route">
    Search weather along a planned route:
    
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Select Route Search">
        Choose the "Route" tab in the search panel.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Enter Waypoints">
        Input waypoints along your planned route separated by spaces or commas.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Set Buffer">
        Define a geographic buffer (in nautical miles) around the route.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Search">
        Click "Search" to retrieve weather for all stations along and near the route.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Review Route Weather">
        View weather conditions sequentially along your planned flight path.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
  
  <Tab title="Free Text">
    Perform open-ended weather searches:
    
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Select Free Text Search">
        Choose the "Free Text" tab.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Enter Search Terms">
        Type any search terms (region names, multiple airports, etc.).
      </Step>
      <Step title="Search">
        The system interprets your query and returns relevant weather data.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Refine Results">
        Use filters to narrow results by weather type or severity.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### Understanding METAR Data

METAR reports contain encoded weather observations. The system displays:

- **Station**: Airport or weather station identifier
- **Time**: Observation time (Zulu/UTC)
- **Wind**: Direction and speed, gusts if present
- **Visibility**: In statute miles or meters
- **Weather Phenomena**: Rain, fog, thunderstorms, etc.
- **Clouds**: Coverage and altitude of cloud layers
- **Temperature/Dewpoint**: In Celsius
- **Altimeter**: Barometric pressure setting
- **Remarks**: Additional information and trends

### Filtering Weather Data

Filter weather results for relevant information:

- **By Weather Class**: Filter NOTAMs by classification
- **By Severity**: Show only significant weather
- **By Time**: Display forecasts for specific time windows
- **By Distance**: For route searches, filter by distance from route

### Refreshing Weather Data

Weather information is time-sensitive:

- Weather data shows the observation time
- Click "Refresh" to get the latest observations
- METAR updates typically every hour or when conditions change
- TAF updates every 6 hours or as needed

<Note>
Always check the observation time. Weather older than 1 hour may not reflect current conditions.
</Note>

## Weight and Balance

Weight and balance calculations are critical for every flight to ensure:

- Aircraft operates within structural weight limits
- Center of gravity remains within approved envelope
- Aircraft handling characteristics are predictable and safe

### Creating a New Weight and Balance Calculation

<Steps>
  <Step title="Access Weight & Balance">
    Navigate to Air Traffic Control > Weight & Balance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="View Existing Calculations">
    The main page displays a table of all previous weight and balance calculations.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start New Calculation">
    Click "New Weight & Balance" to open the calculation form.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select Aircraft">
    Choose the aircraft from the dropdown. The system auto-populates:
    - Basic empty weight
    - Empty center of gravity
    - Empty moment
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the Loading Table">
    Fill in the weight and balance table (see detailed steps below).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review Calculations">
    Verify that all totals are within limits.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save Calculation">
    Save the weight and balance sheet for flight records.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Weight and Balance Table Structure

The weight and balance form is a data-driven table with different row types:

#### Row Types

**Static Rows**: Display labels and headers (not editable)

**Input Rows**: Require data entry, including:
- Pilot weight and seating position
- Passenger weights and positions
- Cargo weights and locations
- Fuel quantities and tank positions

**Subtotal Rows**: Automatically calculated:
- Operating empty weight totals
- Operating with fuel totals
- Ramp weight subtotals

**Total Rows**: Final calculations for:
- Takeoff weight, CG, moment, and MAC
- Landing weight, CG, moment, and MAC

### Completing the Weight and Balance Form

<Steps>
  <Step title="Basic Empty Weight">
    This section auto-populates from aircraft data:
    - Weight empty
    - CG empty
    - Moment empty
  </Step>
  
  <Step title="Fuel Loading">
    Enter fuel information:
    - Weight of fuel (in pounds or kilograms)
    - Arm (distance from datum, usually pre-set)
    - Moment calculates automatically (Weight × Arm)
  </Step>
  
  <Step title="Pilot in Command">
    Enter pilot data:
    - Weight (from crew member profile or manual entry)
    - Arm (seat position arm)
    - Moment auto-calculates
  </Step>
  
  <Step title="Additional Crew">
    For each crew member:
    - Enter weight
    - Specify seating position arm
    - System calculates moment
  </Step>
  
  <Step title="Passengers">
    For each passenger row:
    - Enter passenger weight
    - Specify seat position arm
    - Moment auto-calculates
  </Step>
  
  <Step title="Cargo">
    For each cargo area:
    - Enter cargo weight
    - Specify cargo compartment arm
    - Moment auto-calculates
  </Step>
  
  <Step title="Review Subtotals">
    The system automatically calculates:
    - **Operating Empty Weight**: Basic empty + crew
    - **Operating Fuel Weight**: Operating empty + fuel
    - **Ramp Weight**: All items loaded
  </Step>
  
  <Step title="Takeoff Calculations">
    Review takeoff parameters:
    - **Total Takeoff Weight**: Sum of all weights
    - **Takeoff CG**: Total moment ÷ total weight
    - **Takeoff MAC**: CG position as percentage of mean aerodynamic chord
  </Step>
  
  <Step title="Landing Calculations">
    Review landing parameters (accounting for fuel burn):
    - **Landing Weight**: Takeoff weight - fuel to be burned
    - **Landing CG**: Adjusted for fuel burn
    - **Landing MAC**: Landing CG as percentage of MAC
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Verifying Weight and Balance Limits

After completing the calculation, verify all values against aircraft limits:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Maximum Takeoff Weight">
    Ensure the calculated takeoff weight does not exceed the aircraft's maximum takeoff weight (MTOW). If over, reduce fuel, passengers, or cargo.
  </Accordion>
  
  <Accordion title="Center of Gravity Envelope">
    The takeoff and landing CG must fall within the approved CG envelope for the aircraft. Consult the aircraft flight manual for the envelope chart.
  </Accordion>
  
  <Accordion title="Maximum Landing Weight">
    Verify the landing weight is at or below maximum landing weight (MLW). If over, burn down fuel before landing.
  </Accordion>
  
  <Accordion title="MAC Percentage">
    The MAC percentage should typically be between 15% and 35% for most aircraft. Consult your aircraft's specific limits.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
**CRITICAL SAFETY NOTICE**

Never depart on a flight if:
- Takeoff weight exceeds maximum takeoff weight
- Takeoff or landing CG falls outside the approved envelope
- MAC percentage is outside approved limits

Replan the loading and recalculate. Safety is paramount.
</Warning>

### Adjusting Out-of-Limits Calculations

If calculations are out of limits:

**For Over-Weight Conditions:**
1. Reduce fuel to minimum required plus reserves
2. Reduce passenger count
3. Reduce or redistribute cargo
4. Consider a fuel stop to reduce takeoff fuel

**For CG Out of Envelope:**
1. Redistribute passengers to different seats
2. Move cargo to different compartments
3. Adjust fuel distribution (if multiple tanks)
4. Add ballast in appropriate location (if available)

### Saving and Printing Weight and Balance

Once verified:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Final Review">
    Review all entries for accuracy.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save Calculation">
    Click "Save" to store the weight and balance sheet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Print for Flight">
    Print or export the calculation to PDF.
  </Step>
  <Step title="File with Flight Documents">
    Include the weight and balance sheet with the flight's official documentation.

## Viewing Historical Weight and Balance Records

The Weight and Balance dashboard displays all previous calculations:

### Table Columns

- **Date**: When the calculation was created
- **Aircraft**: Which aircraft the calculation is for
- **Registered By**: Who created the calculation
- **Pilot in Command**: The assigned pilot
- **Takeoff Weight**: Total takeoff weight
- **Takeoff CG**: Center of gravity at takeoff
- **Status**: Whether within limits or flagged for review
- **Actions**: View details, print, or delete

### Searching Historical Records

- **Filter by Aircraft**: View calculations for a specific aircraft
- **Filter by Date Range**: Find calculations within a date range
- **Filter by Pilot**: See calculations for a specific pilot
- **Sort**: Sort by any column to organize data

### Viewing Calculation Details

Click on any calculation row to view:

- Complete weight and balance table
- All calculated subtotals and totals
- Pilot and crew information
- Associated flight transfer (if linked)
- Creation and modification history

## Integration with Flight Operations

Weight and balance calculations integrate with flight operations:

- Link W&B calculations to specific flight transfers
- Auto-populate crew weights from personnel profiles
- Verify crew qualifications before flight
- Automatically update aircraft flight hours after completed missions

## Best Practices

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Check Weather Before Every Flight">
    Always check current weather and forecasts for origin, destination, and alternates before departure. Weather can change rapidly.
  </Accordion>
  
  <Accordion title="Weight and Balance for Every Flight">
    Complete a weight and balance calculation for every flight, even if the loading appears similar to previous flights. Small changes matter.
  </Accordion>
  
  <Accordion title="Conservative Estimates">
    When estimating passenger or cargo weights, round up. It's safer to overestimate weight than underestimate.
  </Accordion>
  
  <Accordion title="Document Everything">
    Keep weight and balance sheets and weather briefings as part of your official flight records for compliance and accident investigation.
  </Accordion>
  
  <Accordion title="Re-check Before Departure">
    If there are any last-minute changes to loading or weather deteriorates, re-do your calculations and weather check.
  </Accordion>
  
  <Accordion title="Know Your Aircraft Limits">
    Memorize your aircraft's maximum weights and CG envelope. Don't rely solely on the system to flag issues.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Weather Data Not Loading">
    If weather data fails to load:
    - Check your internet connection
    - Verify the airport code is correct (ICAO format)
    - Try refreshing the page
    - Weather stations may be temporarily offline
  </Accordion>
  
  <Accordion title="Weight and Balance Calculations Incorrect">
    If calculations seem wrong:
    - Verify aircraft basic empty weight is correct in aircraft profile
    - Check that all arms are entered correctly
    - Ensure weights are in consistent units (all pounds or all kg)
    - Review each row for data entry errors
  </Accordion>
  
  <Accordion title="Cannot Save Calculation">
    If unable to save:
    - Ensure all required fields are completed
    - Check that totals are within aircraft limits (system may prevent saving out-of-limits calculations)
    - Verify you have permission to create weight and balance records
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
